Phase Shift and 2-adic Symmetry

in 3n+/-1 Collatz-like Systems

Abstract. We present a formal 2-adic valuation analysis of the Collatz variants 3n+1 and 3n-1. While both systems exhibit identical average behavior (E[v2] = 2, drift D = -0.415), their modular distributions differ dramatically. For n = 1 (mod 4), 3n+1 shows E[v2] = 3 while 3n-1 shows E[v2] = 1 - a phase shift of Delta = 2. This structural asymmetry explains why 3n+1 contracts strongly for certain residue classes, preventing cycle formation, while 3n-1 admits exactly three cycles.

1. The 2-adic Drift

For an odd integer n in the sequence 3n + k, one step produces:

n_next = (3n + k) / 2^(v2(3n+k))

where v2(m) is the 2-adic valuation - the exponent of the highest power of 2 dividing m.

The drift coefficient measures average contraction:

D = log2(3) - E[v2] = 1.585 - 2.0 = -0.415

Negative drift means the sequence contracts on average.
